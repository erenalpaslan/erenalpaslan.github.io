In the world of developers, we dedicate ourselves to perfecting our application processes to provide a proper user experience. It is not just about making things work; it is also about handling issues gracefully when they arise.

Errors should never pass silently. Unless explicitly silenced. The Zen of Python — Tim Peters

The concept of a Job

Creating a coroutine with the builders launch and async returns an instance of Job. A Job is a cancellable thing holding a lifecycle, and it can be arranged into parent–child hierarchies — so you can combine Jobs and keep control over the lifecycle of the coroutine.

Job states

We have no access to the states directly, but there are related properties: isActive, isCompleted and isCancelled. When all children have completed their work, the parent moves to completed.

In a parent–child hierarchy, cancelling the parent recursively cancels all children. An exception in a child other than CancellationException propagates upward and cancels the parent and its remaining children — which is usually not what you want from one failing request.

SupervisorJob

A SupervisorJob changes that rule in one direction only: a failing child no longer takes down its siblings, while cancelling the parent still cancels everything beneath it.
